Why this format

Three deliberate decisions, each aimed at a specific, documented failure mode.

1

No overnight

The 36-hour format quietly filters out anyone with caregiving duties, health limits, or a job that needs them functional on Sunday. As one developer put it, only "young developers with no responsibilities and ample free time" can participate. That is a design failure, not a skill filter.

Six hours on a Saturday afternoon. Ends at 19:00 sharp. Everyone goes home.

2

No teams

Team matching burns the first 30–60 minutes, produces mismatched roles, and collapses when one person doesn't show. Solo removes all three. It also means a no-show costs one seat instead of one team.

A solo six-hour build wasn't realistic two years ago. v0 and the AI SDK are precisely what changed that — which is why this event makes sense now and not before.

3

Nobody leaves empty-handed

At most hackathons only the winners present and everyone else's work disappears the same day. Here, submissions close and every participant demos at their own desk in a 40-minute gallery walk. Then the top five present for three minutes each.

Every participant receives their score breakdown and written feedback. The rubric is published at the start, not revealed at the end. The single most common complaint about hackathon judging is "I never found out why I lost" — this closes it.

What sponsors get back

Specific, and delivered within one week.

We don't report impressions. The platform we built records completion rate, projects shipped, and which products they were built on as first-class data, because "how many people saw it" is the number sponsors have told us they're tired of receiving.

  • Registered vs. actual attendance — the honest pair, not just the flattering one
  • Number of projects shipped, and how many were built on Vercel products
  • Links to every deployed project, not only the winners
  • Photos and a written recap
  • Confirmed brand placement — logo on the event graphic, mention and description on this page, and a social post

We would also welcome a judge or mentor joining us, remotely or in person. Judging runs 17:30–19:00 KST.

Practical note: this is a first edition at small scale. We'd rather tell you that up front than pad the numbers. What we can promise is that whatever happens gets reported accurately, including the parts that don't flatter us.

Judging rubric

Published to participants before the clock starts.

CriterionWhat we look at
ShippedDoes it open on the internet right now? Submissions are URLs, not demo videos
ConstraintDid it incorporate the condition revealed on the day?
UsefulnessCan you say who uses this and why in one sentence?
Follow-throughWhere did the six hours go? Unfinished work still scores if the path is visible

Judges are chosen to look at different things. Rankings and other judges' scores are hidden from judges during scoring — visible rankings drag scores toward consensus.
